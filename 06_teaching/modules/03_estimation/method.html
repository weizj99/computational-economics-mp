Inputs

The inputs behind every dot.

Watch the 48-second explanation: Sobol points in the chosen range ↗

For year t, read the three targets from Moments.csv and the six bounds from boxes.csv. For candidate i and parameter p ∈ {σ, N, ζ}, map the saved unit coordinate as xitp = lotp + uip × (hitp − lotp). This gives three parameters for one economy; repeat the same mapping for each of the 512 points.

The selected-candidate walkthrough shows the actual u, bounds and mapped parameters. The solver uses M = floor(N), draws productivity with σ and the fixed seed, lets ζ pick φ, and computes the three moments. It then scores those moments against that year's targets.

How ζ sets φ

In the first round of period 1, the solver computes and sorts the firms’ break-even fixed-labor values. For ζ < 1, it selects the first value whose cumulative share is strictly greater than ζ. For ζ ≥ 1, it multiplies the largest value by ζ. That value is φ, and it stays the same in later rounds and in period 2. So ζ is a search coordinate and φ is an outcome of the solve. φ is in model labor units, not a percentage; 1e−6 is one millionth.

Source: qmp/equilibrium.py::_solve_period. Every candidate uses this rule.

Design and checks
  • 512 points fixed in advance in each year’s search box.
  • Coordinates: productivity dispersion σ, continuous N, fixed-cost control ζ.
  • Lower corner included; upper corner excluded.
  • Candidates with the same M share the corresponding random inputs.
  • All 22,528 records must be valid.
  • Selection sorts by (loss, row_id).
  • The selected points are checked against the annual winners table.
