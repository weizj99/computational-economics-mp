Scope & notation
  • This sector example has no entry or aggregate labor clearing.
  • The full course adds nonconstant returns and two periods.
  • Edmond’s within-sector γ is our η; our γ denotes returns to scale.
  • Trottner’s within-sector ρ is our η; the course’s ρ governs productivity persistence.
  • Its constants are for the example only; the course’s model uses its own values.

Try it yourself

Solve the default sector on your computer.

Download the Python example and run it in the folder where you saved it. It needs Python 3.10 or later with NumPy, and takes under a second. In the course repository, run python lessons/00_prerequisites/ab08.py from the root instead.

python ab08.py

It solves the page’s default sector, where A has an advantage and θ = 1.5, first under Cournot and then under Bertrand conduct, and prints each firm’s share, elasticity, markup and price. Add --self-test to run the checks first.

Check your result

Under Cournot, firm 1 (A on this page) has share 0.595718, elasticity 2.092042, markup 1.915716 and price 1.197322; the page shows 59.57%, 2.092, 1.916 and 1.197. Firms 2 and 3 have shares of 25.98% and 14.44%. Choose Bertrand on the page to compare the second table: firm 1’s share is 66.87% and its markup 1.603. With --self-test, the first line reads “AB08 checks passed: monopoly, symmetry, both conduct rules, residuals.”
