The model

Parameters

θ, η and ρ are the same in every year. Unit 03 estimates σ, N and ζ for each year.

θElasticity across markets
How easily spending moves from one market to another. Each market is one sector. Fixed at 1.13125. 00 · AB08: demand
ηElasticity within a market
How easily buyers switch among the firms of one market; η > θ > 1. Fixed at 4.36875. It also sets the lowest possible markup, η/(η − 1) = 1.297, which a firm with a tiny market share charges. 00 · AB08: demand
ρPersistence of productivity
How much of a firm’s log productivity carries over from period 1 to period 2. Fixed at 0.9. Period 1 is drawn from the stationary distribution of this process, so productivity has the same distribution in both periods. 02.1 · The economy is stationary
σProductivity dispersion
The standard deviation of the random shocks to log productivity. In period 1, log productivity has standard deviation σ/√(1 − ρ2). Estimated for each year. 02.3 · Parameters to moments
NPotential firms
The number of potential firms per market, a continuous number in the search. Estimated for each year. 03 · From a coordinate to an economy
MPotential firms in the solved economy
M = floor(N), the whole number of potential firms in each market. Entry and exit decide how many of them operate. 02.1 · Entry and exit
JMarkets
The number of markets in one economy: 10,000. Firm arrays have M rows and J columns.
ζFixed-cost control
Before any firm exits in period 1, ζ picks φ from the firms’ break-even values: for ζ < 1, about a share ζ of the firms have break-even values below φ; for ζ ≥ 1, φ is ζ times the largest. ζ can exceed 1 because exit raises the profits of the firms that stay. Estimated for each year. 02.3 · How ζ picks φ
φFixed labor
The labor each operating firm needs before it produces anything, in model labor units; the pages show it in millionths. ζ picks it before any firm exits in period 1, and it stays the same in every exit round and in period 2. 02.3 · How ζ picks φ
ψSGA, ψCOGSLabor shares of costs
The shares of labor in SGA costs and in the cost of goods sold, both 0.33. They turn wage bills into costs: SGA = Wφ/ψSGA. 02.3 · Build the three moments
γ, αCOGSTechnology
Returns to scale, 1.0252, and the elasticity of output with respect to the cost of goods sold, 0.8365. The elasticity with respect to capital is their difference. 02.3 · Build the three moments
φ̄, ϕLabor supply
The scale and the elasticity of household labor supply, 1 and 0.25: households supply φ̄Wϕ units of labor at the wage W. 02.2 · Cournot and equilibrium
Solving one economy

Firms and markets

What the solver finds for given parameters and random draws.

sMarket share
A firm’s share of the sales in its own market. 02.2 · Cournot and equilibrium
εElasticity a firm faces
1/ε = s/θ + (1 − s)/η. A firm with a tiny share faces about η; a firm with the whole market faces θ. 02.2 · Cournot and equilibrium
μMarkup
Price over marginal cost, μ = ε/(ε − 1). It rises with the firm’s market share. 00 · AB08: competition
CournotCournot competition
Firms choose quantities, and prices follow from demand. A firm with a larger share has more pricing power, so shares, markups and prices must be solved together: the share fixed point. 00 · AB08: competition
W, YWage and output
The wage and the economy’s output; the price of output is 1. They adjust until the labor firms use equals the labor households supply. 02.2 · Cournot and equilibrium
n, eLabor and employment
n is a firm’s production labor. Its employment is e = n + φ while it operates and 0 otherwise. 02.3 · Build the three moments
OperatingOperating, entering, exiting
Of the M potential firms in a market, the operating firms produce in a period. Between the two periods a firm can continue, enter (operate only in period 2) or exit (operate only in period 1). 02.1 · Entry and exit
RoundExit round
One pass of the entry and exit calculation: solve the economy, compare each firm’s profit with a cutoff for the whole economy, and remove the firms at or below it. The rounds stop when fewer than three exits are proposed. Rounds are solver steps, not dates. 02.1 · Entry and exit
PeriodTwo periods
Each economy is solved for two periods. Firms keep their identity and φ; each firm’s productivity changes, but its distribution across firms does not, because the economy is stationary. The two periods give the employment reallocation moment; they are not two calendar years. 02.1 · Entry and exit
StationaryStationary economy

Period 1’s productivity is drawn from the stationary distribution of the AR(1) process for log productivity, and period 2 takes one step of it.

The distribution of productivity across firms, φ and the totals are therefore almost the same in both periods, while each firm’s productivity, size and operating status can change. These changes make up employment reallocation.

The three moments

Computed the same way in the model and in the data.

REALLEmployment reallocation
(JC + JD)/[½(E1 + E2)]: the jobs created and destroyed between the two periods, over average employment. 02.3 · Build the three moments
Et, JC, JDEmployment and job flows
Et is total employment in period t. Job creation JC adds up firms’ increases in employment; job destruction JD adds up their decreases. JC − JD = E2 − E1. 02.3 · Build the three moments
μ̄Sales-weighted markup
The average markup of the operating firms in period 1, each weighted by its share w of all sales in the economy. 02.3 · Build the three moments
SGASelling, general and administrative costs
Overhead costs. In the model they are the wage bill of fixed labor divided by ψSGA. 02.3 · Build the three moments
SGA / TCSGA share of total cost
Each operating firm’s SGA over its total cost in period 1, averaged with sales weights. It is not total SGA over total cost. 02.3 · Build the three moments
TargetsTargets
The data’s values of the three moments in a year. 03 · Read the targets
Units 01, 03 and 05

Estimation

Choosing parameters so that the model’s moments match the data’s.

MomentMoment
A statistic computed the same way in the data and in the model, such as a variance or an average. 01 · Moment matching
GMMGeneralized method of moments
Choose the parameters whose model moments, calculated directly, come closest to the data’s. 01 · Moment matching
SMMSimulated method of moments
The same, with the model’s moments computed from S simulated data sets. A larger S reduces the simulation error, not the sampling error of the data. 01 · Moment matching
CRNCommon random numbers
The same random draws for every parameter candidate, so that differences between candidates come from the parameters, not from new draws. 01 · Keep the random draws
BoxSearch box
Each year’s lower and upper limits for σ, N and ζ. 03 · Read the targets and ranges
SobolSobol points
A fixed sequence of points that spreads evenly over a box. The course maps the same 512 three-dimensional points into each year’s box to get 512 candidates for (σ, N, ζ). 03 · From a coordinate to an economy
LossWeighted loss
The sum of the three squared gaps between model and data, each multiplied by a weight: the inverse of that moment’s sample variance over the 44 years. The candidate with the lowest loss is the year’s estimate. 03 · Turn the gaps into one score
Unit 04

Computing

Making the same calculation take less time.

WorkerWorker process
A separate program that solves its own economies. In the course each worker uses one linear-algebra thread. 04.01 · Parallel work
ShardShard
A share of the candidates that one process solves; no two shards share a candidate. 04.01 · Parallel work
SpeedupSpeedup and efficiency
With W workers, the speedup is T(1)/T(W) and the efficiency is T(1)/(W × T(W)), where T is the time to finish the same work. 04.01 · Parallel work
VectorizeVectorization
One array operation over all firms and markets instead of a loop that calls a function once per market. 04.02 · Vectorization
B × M × JBatch
B economies with the same M, stacked into one array so that the GPU works on all of them at once. 04.03 · GPU batching
KernelKernel
A function that runs on the GPU; each call is a launch. Fusing kernels combines several operations into one launch, so that intermediate arrays need not be written to memory. 04.04 · Kernel fusion
ResidentResident loop
An iteration that runs on the GPU together with its stopping checks, without returning to the host after each step. 04.05 · Resident iteration
ProfileProfiling
Measuring where the computing time goes before changing the code. 04.06 · Profiling
FP32, FP64Single and double precision
Floating-point numbers with about 7 and about 16 significant digits. The course checks its answers in FP64. 04.07 · Mixed precision
MixedMixed precision
FP32 only for a first guess of the market shares; the correction and the rest of the economy stay in FP64. 04.07 · Mixed precision
TritonTriton
A language for writing GPU kernels in Python. The optional lessons of unit 04 use it. 04 · Contents
Warm-upFair comparison
Time the same checked work with the same clock, after a warm-up, and keep every repeated time. 04.08 · Fair comparison
